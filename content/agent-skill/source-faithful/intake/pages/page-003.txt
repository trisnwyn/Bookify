Three levels of reading
Three levels keep a description honest.
  Detail. What is visible without interpretation.
  Setting. What the detail means in this place and sequence.
  Warrant. The evidence that supports the reading.
The three levels answer different questions: What is here? What does it mean here? Why should the
reading be accepted?
A reading can be correct at one level and unsupported at another. A visible detail is not yet a setting; a
setting is not yet a warrant. In the case of the two labels, the labels are details. The order of the labels
is part of the setting. The recorded order is the warrant for reading them as a sequence rather than a
contradiction.
These notes use the three terms throughout.
